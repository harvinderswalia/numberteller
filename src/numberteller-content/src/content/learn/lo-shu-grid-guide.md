The Lo Shu grid gives you a visual summary of a client's date of birth. It shows which numbers are strong, which are absent and which lines of energy are complete. This guide covers how to build the grid, how to read each layer and how to structure a full consultation around it.

## Building the grid

The Lo Shu is a 3×3 magic square. Every row, column and diagonal adds to 15. Each digit from 1 to 9 has a fixed position:

```
4 | 9 | 2
---------
3 | 5 | 7
---------
8 | 1 | 6
```

To plot a chart, write out the full date of birth as DD MM YYYY. Take every digit and place it in its box. In this library's convention, zeros are not plotted, and repeated digits are written as many times as they occur. A date with two 1s shows "11" in the bottom-centre box.

Always draw the empty grid first, then fill it digit by digit. Tick each digit off the date as you place it. This simple habit prevents the most common plotting error: missing a digit from the year.

### The driver and conductor variation

Many Indian practitioners add two more numbers to the grid. These are the driver (the day of birth reduced to a single digit) and the conductor (the Life Path number). Both are plotted on top of the date digits.

This variation can change a reading significantly, as the worked example below shows. Neither approach is wrong. Choose one convention, state it in your report and apply it to every client.

## Repeated digits

A digit that appears once is generally read as present and balanced. Twice usually indicates emphasis. Three or more times is often read as excess, where the quality becomes overactive or hard to manage.

Schools differ on the precise meaning of each count. Some practitioners have detailed interpretations for one, two, three and four occurrences of every digit. Others simply note "strong" or "overloaded". Our companion article on [reading zeros and repeated digits in the Lo Shu grid](/learn/lo-shu-grid-zeros-and-repeats) covers this in detail.

## Missing numbers

A missing number is a digit that does not appear in the date at all. In Lo Shu practice, missing numbers point to qualities the client may need to develop consciously. They are often discussed as lessons rather than flaws.

Brief working themes, as commonly taught:

| Number | Commonly associated theme |
|---|---|
| 1 | Self-expression, communication, independence |
| 2 | Sensitivity, intuition, cooperation |
| 3 | Imagination, memory, creative thinking |
| 4 | Order, method, practical organisation |
| 5 | Balance, emotional centre, adaptability |
| 6 | Home, responsibility, creativity in the material world |
| 7 | Reflection, learning through experience |
| 8 | Attention to detail, financial and practical management |
| 9 | Ambition, idealism, humanitarian drive |

Do not present a long list of absences as a problem. Frame them as areas for growth.

## Arrows (strength and missing)

An arrow is any line of three boxes: a row, a column or a diagonal. There are eight lines in total.

- **Arrow of strength:** all three boxes in the line contain at least one digit.
- **Arrow of weakness (missing arrow):** all three boxes in the line are empty.
- **Partial line:** one or two boxes filled. Most lines in most charts are partial, and they are not usually named as arrows.

Strength arrows describe natural, reliable capacities. Missing arrows describe areas where the client may need conscious effort, support or structure. Be careful with language here. "Missing arrow of action" is a technical term, not a judgement of the client.

## Planes

The planes give names to the eight lines. Naming varies by school, so check your own training and keep it consistent. The convention used across this library is:

| Line | Digits | Common name |
|---|---|---|
| Top row | 4-9-2 | Mental / thought plane |
| Middle row | 3-5-7 | Emotional / soul plane |
| Bottom row | 8-1-6 | Practical / physical plane |
| Left column | 4-3-8 | Thought (planning) |
| Middle column | 9-5-1 | Will (determination) |
| Right column | 2-7-6 | Action |
| Diagonal | 4-5-6 | Determination / success |
| Diagonal | 2-5-8 | Compassion / spirituality, sometimes called the Raj Yog arrow |

Note that 5 sits on four lines: the middle row, middle column and both diagonals. A missing 5 therefore leaves four lines unable to complete. A present 5 supports all of them.

## Worked example: Priya Sharma, 15-08-1990

Priya Sharma is a fictional sample client, born 15 August 1990.

### Step 1: list the digits

Date: 15 08 1990 → digits 1, 5, 0, 8, 1, 9, 9, 0.

Remove the zeros. Plotted digits: 1, 5, 8, 1, 9, 9.

| Digit | Count |
|---|---|
| 1 | 2 |
| 5 | 1 |
| 8 | 1 |
| 9 | 2 |
| 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 | 0 (missing) |

### Step 2: draw the grid

```
  -  | 99  |  -
---------------
  -  |  5  |  -
---------------
  8  | 11  |  -
```

### Step 3: check all eight lines

| Line | Boxes filled | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 4-9-2 | 9 only | Partial |
| 3-5-7 | 5 only | Partial |
| 8-1-6 | 8, 1 | Partial |
| 4-3-8 | 8 only | Partial |
| 9-5-1 | 9, 5, 1 | **Complete — arrow of strength (will)** |
| 2-7-6 | none | **Empty — missing arrow (action)** |
| 4-5-6 | 5 only | Partial |
| 2-5-8 | 5, 8 | Partial |

So Priya has one arrow of strength, the 9-5-1 will plane, and one missing arrow, the 2-7-6 action line.

### Step 4: interpret

Within Lo Shu practice, a complete 9-5-1 line is associated with determination and persistence. The empty 2-7-6 line is often read as a gap between intention and follow-through. In a consultation you might explore whether Priya has strong resolve but sometimes delays acting on it.

The doubled 1 and doubled 9 suggest emphasis on self-expression and on ambition or idealism. Five missing numbers (2, 3, 4, 6, 7) give you material for a development conversation.

### Step 5: the variation changes the picture

Priya's driver is 15 → 1 + 5 = 6. Her Life Path by the library's component method is 15/6, so her conductor is also 6. If you plot both, she gains two 6s.

| Line | Date only | With driver + conductor |
|---|---|---|
| 8-1-6 | Partial | **Complete** |
| 2-7-6 | Empty | Partial |
| Missing numbers | 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 | 2, 3, 4, 7 |

Her missing arrow of action disappears, and a practical-plane arrow appears. That is a material change to the reading.

There is a second trap. By the full-digit method, Priya's Life Path is 1+5+0+8+1+9+9+0 = 33, a master number. A practitioner using that method would plot a different conductor. This is why your [Life Path calculation method](/learn/how-to-calculate-life-path-number) and your grid convention must both be fixed and documented.

## Structuring the reading

A consistent sequence keeps the reading clear and your notes comparable.

1. **Confirm the date.** Check the date of birth against an official document where possible. A wrong day or year changes the whole grid.
2. **State your convention.** Note whether you plot date digits only, or add driver and conductor.
3. **Plot and count.** Draw the grid, tick off each digit and record the count per box.
4. **Name the present numbers.** Start with what is there, including repeats and their intensity.
5. **Name the missing numbers.** Present them as areas to develop, not deficits.
6. **Check all eight lines.** Mark each as complete, empty or partial. List arrows of strength first.
7. **Connect to the core chart.** Compare the grid with the Life Path, Birthday and name numbers. Look for themes that agree or contrast.
8. **Close with practical guidance.** Give the client two or three concrete points to work with.

Step 1 matters more than it seems. Many [common numerology calculation errors](/learn/common-numerology-calculation-errors) start with a transcription mistake in the date.

## Key points

- Plot every non-zero digit of the full date; show repeats by repetition.
- There are eight lines. A full line is an arrow of strength; an empty line is a missing arrow.
- Plane names vary by school. Pick one naming set and keep it.
- Adding driver and conductor can create or remove arrows. Document whether you use it.
- Priya Sharma (15-08-1990) has a 9-5-1 arrow of strength and an empty 2-7-6 line on the date-only grid.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I plot the century digits of the year?

Yes. In this library's convention, every non-zero digit of DD MM YYYY is plotted, including the 1 and 9 of 1990. Some practitioners use only part of the year, but that is a separate convention.

### Is a missing number always a weakness?

No. It is usually read as a quality to develop consciously. Present numbers and complete arrows elsewhere in the grid often offer compensating strengths.

### Should I add the driver and conductor?

That depends on your training. It is widely used in Indian practice and changes results, as Priya's example shows. Whichever you choose, apply it to every client and state it in the report.
