Zeros and repeated digits cause more inconsistency in Lo Shu readings than any other part of the grid. Two practitioners can plot the same date and describe it differently simply because they treat repeats differently. This article sets out the common conventions, shows how they play out on real date patterns and explains how to document your own approach.

## Zeros

### Why zeros are not plotted

The Lo Shu square contains nine boxes, one for each digit from 1 to 9. There is no position for 0. In this library's convention, and in most Lo Shu practice, zeros in a date of birth are simply skipped when plotting.

Some practitioners still mention zeros in the reading. They may describe a date with many zeros as having fewer "active" digits, or speak of 0 as amplifying the numbers beside it. These are interpretive additions, not part of plotting. If you use them, say so, and keep them separate from the grid itself.

### Dates in the 2000s

Clients born from 2000 onwards often have dates full of zeros. The year itself usually contributes at least two. Add a day such as 10 or 20 and a month such as 01, 10 or 02, and the grid becomes sparse.

Consider a fictional client born on 10-10-2010.

| Step | Working |
|---|---|
| Full date | 1 0 1 0 2 0 1 0 |
| Zeros removed | 4 zeros skipped |
| Plotted digits | 1, 1, 2, 1 |
| Counts | 1 × 3, 2 × 1 |
| Missing | 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 |

```
  -  |  -  |  2
---------------
  -  |  -  |  -
---------------
  -  | 111 |  -
```

Only two boxes are filled. No line is complete. Three lines are fully empty: the 3-5-7 emotional plane, the 4-3-8 thought column and the 4-5-6 diagonal.

### How to read a sparse grid

A grid like this needs careful handling in a consultation. Seven missing numbers can sound alarming if you list them one by one. Instead, focus on what is present and on how strongly it is present.

Here, the triple 1 dominates. That is the main story of the grid. The missing numbers become a short list of development themes, prioritised by what matters to the client now. For a child's chart, keep the tone especially gentle and practical.

This is also a strong case for the driver and conductor variation, if your school uses it. The client's driver is 10 → 1, and the Life Path by the component method is 1 + 1 + 3 = 5. Adding a 5 would fill the centre box. Whether you do that is a convention choice, covered in our [Lo Shu grid practitioner's guide](/learn/lo-shu-grid-guide).

## Repeated digits

A repeat is any digit that appears more than once in the date. You show it by writing the digit as many times as it occurs: 11, 999 and so on. The count is then read as intensity.

### Single, double, triple and more

The following are general tendencies as commonly taught. Schools vary, and some have detailed meanings for each count of each digit.

| Count | Common reading |
|---|---|
| Once | Present and balanced. The quality is available and usually well expressed. |
| Twice | Emphasised. The quality is strong and noticeable to others. |
| Three times | Heavy. Often read as excess, where the quality becomes harder to manage. |
| Four or more | Overloaded. The quality may dominate or turn inward, depending on the school. |

Some traditions treat a double as the healthiest form of certain numbers. Others read every repeat beyond one as imbalance. Neither is more correct; they are different teaching lines.

### Digit-specific examples

As a general pattern, a repeated 1 is associated with strong self-expression, and at higher counts with difficulty saying what one feels. A repeated 9 is linked with ambition and idealism, and at higher counts with impatience towards others. A repeated 2 is associated with sensitivity, and in excess with being easily hurt.

Use these as starting points, not scripts. The rest of the chart should shape how you speak about any repeat.

## Reading intensity

Intensity is about proportion, not just count. A double 1 means something different in a grid with six filled boxes than in a grid with two.

Ask three questions for each repeated digit:

1. **How many times does it appear?** Record the raw count.
2. **What else is present?** A repeat in a full grid is one strong voice among many. In a sparse grid, it may be the only voice.
3. **Which lines does it sit on?** A heavy digit on a complete arrow reinforces that arrow. A heavy digit on an otherwise empty line suggests concentrated energy without support.

The 5 deserves special attention. It sits on four lines. A repeated 5 therefore influences more of the grid than a repeated corner digit.

## Examples

### Example 1: 22-02-2002 (fictional)

| Step | Working |
|---|---|
| Full date | 2 2 0 2 2 0 0 2 |
| Zeros removed | 3 zeros skipped |
| Plotted digits | 2, 2, 2, 2, 2 |
| Counts | 2 × 5 |
| Missing | 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 |

Only the top-right box is filled, with five 2s. Five of the eight lines are fully empty: 3-5-7, 8-1-6, 4-3-8, 9-5-1 and 4-5-6. The only lines touched at all are those running through the 2 box.

This is an extreme case. Your reading would centre on the 2 — sensitivity, intuition and cooperation — and on the risk of overload. It is a clear example of why intensity must be read in proportion to the whole grid.

## Documenting your convention

Write your rules down once and apply them to every chart. A short convention note in your report template might read:

- Zeros: not plotted; not interpreted.
- Repeats: shown by repetition; read as 1 = balanced, 2 = emphasised, 3+ = excess.
- Driver and conductor: not added (or: added, and marked in a different colour).
- Plane names: as listed in the practice handbook.

This protects you when a client compares your reading with another practitioner's. It also makes your own notes consistent across years. Inconsistent rules are a frequent source of [common numerology calculation errors](/learn/common-numerology-calculation-errors).

If you also calculate the Life Path for the conductor, fix that method too. The [component and full-digit Life Path methods](/learn/how-to-calculate-life-path-number) can give different results for the same date.

## Key points

- The Lo Shu has no box for 0, so zeros are skipped when plotting.
- Dates from 2000 onwards often produce sparse grids; read what is present first.
- Repeats are read as intensity: once balanced, twice emphasised, three or more often excess.
- Read intensity in proportion to the whole grid and the lines a digit sits on.
- Write down your zero, repeat and variation rules, and apply them every time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I ever plot a zero?

Not in the standard Lo Shu square, which has no position for it. If your school interprets zeros, treat that as a separate note outside the grid.

### Is a triple digit always negative?

No. It is commonly read as excess, but how that excess shows depends on the rest of the chart. Some schools treat certain triples as a concentrated talent.

### How do I explain a very empty grid to a client?

Start with the numbers that are present and their strength. Then present two or three missing numbers as development themes, rather than reading out every absence.
