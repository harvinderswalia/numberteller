Every comparison in numerology rests on one question: do these two numbers get along? Whether you are checking a client's Expression against their Life Path or comparing two partners, you need a consistent answer. This guide explains friendly-number tables, the three harmony levels and how harmony feeds into name and compatibility work.

## Why harmony matters

A number never acts alone in a chart. A Life Path describes direction, an Expression describes how a person acts and a Soul Urge describes what they inwardly want. When these numbers sit comfortably together, the chart is usually read as integrated. When they pull in different directions, the reading often turns to inner tension.

Harmony checking gives that judgement a structure. Instead of relying on impression, you look each pair up in a table and record the result.

## Friendly-number tables differ by school

There is no single, universal friendship table. Different traditions publish different ones, and you should know which one you use.

- **Group-based schemes** gather numbers by shared temperament, such as expressive, practical or independent.
- **Planet-based schemes**, common in Indian numerology, assign each number a ruling planet (for example 1 to the Sun, 2 to the Moon). Friend, neutral and enemy relationships then follow the astrological relationships between those planets.
- **Teacher-specific tables** blend these approaches or add exceptions from experience.

These tables do not always agree. A pair that one school calls friendly may be neutral or even unfriendly in another. As with every convention in this library, choose one table, document it and apply it to every client.

## One widely used scheme

The table below shows the friendly groups NumberTeller uses. It is one widely used scheme, not the only valid one.

| Number | Friendly group |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1, 2, 3, 5, 7 |
| 2 | 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9 |
| 3 | 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 9 |
| 4 | 2, 4, 6, 7, 8 |
| 5 | 1, 3, 5, 7 |
| 6 | 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9 |
| 7 | 1, 4, 5, 7 |
| 8 | 2, 4, 6, 8 |
| 9 | 2, 3, 6, 9 |

The table is symmetric: if 3 lists 6, then 6 lists 3, so you can start from either number. To check 3 against 6, find row 3 and see whether 6 is listed. It is, so the pair is friendly.

Notice some broad patterns. 2, 3 and 6 have the widest friendly groups, in keeping with their traditional association with cooperation, sociability and care. 8 has a narrower group, linked with the even, structured numbers. 5 and 7 share a group with 1, reflecting independent, questioning temperaments.

## The three harmony levels

Harmony in this scheme has three levels:

| Level | When it applies | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Perfect** | The two numbers are the same | 1.0 |
| **Friendly** | The two numbers are in each other's friendly group | 0.8 |
| **Neutral** | Neither of the above | 0.5 |

Two points need care.

**Compare roots.** Master numbers are compared by their root for harmony: 11/2 counts as 2, 22/4 as 4 and 33/6 as 6. Discuss the master number separately in your reading, because its meaning goes beyond the root.

**Neutral is not hostile.** This scheme has no "enemy" level. A Neutral pair simply lacks a natural affinity. Planet-based schemes may include enemy relationships; if yours does, explain them gently.

## Harmony within one chart

Within a single chart, the pairs most often checked are:

- **LP–EX:** does the way the client acts suit the direction of their life?
- **LP–SU:** do their inner motivations fit their life direction?
- **EX–SU:** does outer behaviour match inner desire?

In name-correction work, the Birthday number (BD) is often added, giving six pairs in total. Since BD and LP cannot change, the aim is usually to choose an Expression and Soul Urge that harmonise with both. Watch for [over-energy](/learn/over-energy-in-numerology), though. A Perfect match on every pair may mean the same number repeated across the chart.

## Harmony between two people

For compatibility, you compare each person's number with the same position in the other person's chart. One weighted approach uses:

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---|
| Life Path | 40% |
| Expression | 30% |
| Soul Urge | 30% |

Multiply each harmony value by its weight and add them. The weighted harmony then falls between 0.5 (all Neutral) and 1.0 (all Perfect). To turn that into a score out of 100:

**Score = (weighted harmony − 0.5) × 2 × 100**

This rescales the range so that all-Neutral gives 0, all-Friendly gives 60 and all-Perfect gives 100. The score is a way to organise the conversation, not a verdict on the relationship.

## Worked example: Kabir Joshi and Suresh Das

Kabir Joshi and Suresh Das are fictional business partners. Kabir was born on 1 January 1986 and Suresh on 12 June 1988. This library's house conventions apply: date parts and name parts are reduced separately, keeping 11, 22 and 33.

### Step 1: Kabir Joshi's core numbers

- **Life Path:** day 1; month 1; year 1986 → 24 → 6. Then 1 + 1 + 6 = **8**. Full-digit check: 1+1+1+9+8+6 = 26 → 8. Both agree.
- **Expression:** KABIR (K2 A1 B2 I9 R9) = 23 → 5; JOSHI (J1 O6 S1 H8 I9) = 25 → 7. Then 5 + 7 = 12 → **3**.
- **Soul Urge:** KABIR (A1 I9) = 10 → 1; JOSHI (O6 I9) = 15 → 6. Then 1 + 6 = **7**.

### Step 2: Suresh Das's core numbers

- **Life Path:** day 12 → 3; month 6; year 1988 → 26 → 8. Then 3 + 6 + 8 = 17 → **8**. Full-digit check: 1+2+0+6+1+9+8+8 = 35 → 8. Both agree.
- **Expression:** SURESH (S1 U3 R9 E5 S1 H8) = 27 → 9; DAS (D4 A1 S1) = 6. Then 9 + 6 = 15 → **6**.
- **Soul Urge:** SURESH (U3 E5) = 8; DAS (A1) = 1. Then 8 + 1 = **9**.

### Step 3: harmony per dimension

| Dimension | Kabir | Suresh | Level | Value | Weight | Weighted |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Life Path | 8 | 8 | Perfect | 1.0 | 0.4 | 0.40 |
| Expression | 3 | 6 | Friendly | 0.8 | 0.3 | 0.24 |
| Soul Urge | 7 | 9 | Neutral | 0.5 | 0.3 | 0.15 |
| **Total** | | | | | | **0.79** |

### Step 4: the score

(0.79 − 0.5) × 2 × 100 = **58 out of 100**.

### Step 5: interpret

The shared Life Path 8 is the anchor. Both partners are traditionally associated with ambition and material focus, which suits a business. The Expressions, 3 and 6, are friendly: Kabir's expressive style and Suresh's responsible, service-minded style can complement each other with clients.

The Soul Urges, 7 and 9, are neutral. Kabir's inner 7 is linked with analysis and privacy, while Suresh's 9 is linked with idealism and wider purpose. That is the conversation to have: what each partner wants from the business beyond results.

One more check. Both partners carry 8 as their Life Path. A combined chart with doubled 8 may lean towards overwork or control, so it is worth asking how they share authority.

### Step 6: harmony inside each chart

It also helps to look at each partner individually.

| Pair | Kabir | Level | Suresh | Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LP–EX | 8–3 | Neutral | 8–6 | Friendly |
| LP–SU | 8–7 | Neutral | 8–9 | Neutral |
| EX–SU | 3–7 | Neutral | 6–9 | Friendly |

Suresh's chart is more internally harmonious. Kabir's three Neutral pairs suggest a person whose outer style, inner needs and life direction each ask for different things. That is not a flaw, but it may explain why he sometimes feels pulled in several directions.

## Using harmony well

### In name correction

When testing spelling variants, check each new Expression and Soul Urge against the fixed BD and LP. Prefer variants that move Neutral pairs to Friendly without creating over-energy. See [Pythagorean name correction](/learn/name-correction-pythagorean-method) for the full method.

### In compatibility readings

Lead with Perfect and Friendly dimensions, then treat Neutral ones as topics to discuss. For structuring the full session, see [numerology compatibility readings](/learn/numerology-compatibility-readings).

### Keep your table fixed

Changing your friendship table between clients makes scores impossible to compare. If you switch schools, note the date and re-score any chart you revisit.

## Key points

- Friendly-number tables vary by school; group-based and planet-based schemes do not always agree.
- This scheme uses three levels: Perfect (same, 1.0), Friendly (in the group, 0.8) and Neutral (0.5).
- Compare master numbers by their root, but interpret them separately.
- Within a chart, check LP–EX, LP–SU and EX–SU, plus BD in name correction.
- One compatibility weighting is LP 40%, EX 30%, SU 30%, rescaled so all-Neutral = 0 and all-Perfect = 100.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Neutral pair a bad sign?

No. Neutral means no natural affinity, not conflict. It often marks the most useful topic in a reading.

### Can I use a planet-based friendship table instead?

Yes. Many Indian practitioners do. Use it consistently, and do not mix it with a group-based table in the same reading.

### Why is a shared number not always ideal?

A shared number is Perfect for harmony, but it can concentrate one vibration. Check for over-energy across both charts before calling it the strongest result.
