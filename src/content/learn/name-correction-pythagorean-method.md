Name correction is one of the most requested services in a numerology practice, and one of the easiest to get wrong. A single added letter can move a client's Expression number by several places. This guide sets out a working framework for Pythagorean name correction, shows how small spelling changes behave, and covers how to present options so the client stays in control.

## When name correction helps (and when to leave a name alone)

Name correction is a tool for fine-tuning, not a cure for every difficulty. It is most useful when the name numbers clash sharply with the birth numbers, or when one number dominates the whole chart.

Consider it when:

- The Expression (EX) or Soul Urge (SU) sits in obvious tension with the Life Path (LP) and Birthday (BD) under the friendship table you use.
- One number is heavily overloaded across the name and the date.
- The client is already changing their name for another reason, such as marriage, a professional brand or a new venture.

Leave the name alone when it already harmonises reasonably well. Also hold back when the client is anxious and looking for a single fix for every problem. In that case, the reading itself is the service. A good practitioner says "your name is working for you" as readily as "here are some options".

## The harmony framework (BD, LP, EX, SU)

The framework compares four core numbers. The two birth numbers are fixed. The two name numbers can be adjusted.

| Code | Number | Source | Can it change? |
|---|---|---|---|
| BD | Birthday | Day of birth | No |
| LP | Life Path | Full date of birth | No |
| EX | Expression | All letters of the name | Yes |
| SU | Soul Urge | Vowels of the name | Yes |

Your task is to judge how EX and SU sit alongside BD and LP. Schools differ on which numbers are "friendly", "neutral" or "challenging" to one another. Choose one friendship table, write it into your practice notes and apply it to every client. Mixing tables between clients is a common source of contradictory advice.

Two conventions need settling before you calculate anything:

1. **Reduction order.** This library reduces each part of the name separately, then adds the parts and reduces again. Some practitioners total every letter at once. Usually the result is the same, but not always.
2. **The letter Y.** Decide when Y counts as a vowel for Soul Urge, and record it. See our guide on [treating Y as a vowel or consonant](/learn/y-vowel-or-consonant-numerology).

Look up letter values from the standard [Pythagorean number table](/learn/pythagorean-number-table) every time rather than from memory.

### Worked example: the starting chart

Our fictional client is **Nikhil Mehra**, born 10 March 1990.

**Birth numbers**

- BD: day 10 → 1 + 0 = 1.
- LP: day 10 → 1; month 3; year 1990 → 19 → 10 → 1. Then 1 + 3 + 1 = **5**.
- Cross-check by the full-digit method: 1+0+0+3+1+9+9+0 = 23 → 5. Both methods agree here.

**Expression (all letters)**

| Name part | Letters → values | Total | Reduced |
|---|---|---|---|
| NIKHIL | N5 I9 K2 H8 I9 L3 | 36 | 9 |
| MEHRA | M4 E5 H8 R9 A1 | 27 | 9 |
| **EX** | 9 + 9 = 18 | | **9** |

Totalling every letter at once gives 63 → 9, so both reduction orders agree.

**Soul Urge (vowels A, E, I, O, U)**

| Name part | Vowels → values | Total | Reduced |
|---|---|---|---|
| NIKHIL | I9 I9 | 18 | 9 |
| MEHRA | E5 A1 | 6 | 6 |
| **SU** | 9 + 6 = 15 | | **6** |

So the core chart reads BD 1, LP 5, EX 9, SU 6.

## Over-energy (too many of one number)

Over-energy describes a chart where one number appears so often that its qualities tip from strength into excess. Check it before you look at harmony, because it often explains what the client is actually feeling.

Count the letter values in Nikhil Mehra's full name:

| Value | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Count | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 3 |

The number 9 appears three times among the letters. It is also the reduced value of the first name, the surname, the full Expression and the first-name vowels. That is a strong concentration of 9 energy. In Pythagorean numerology, 9 is associated with idealism and completion. In excess, practitioners often read it as scattered giving or difficulty with closure.

Your judgement here matters more than any rule. Some practitioners treat three of one value as normal. Others flag it as over-energy, especially when the same number also reduces from several name parts. Pick a threshold, document it and apply it the same way for every client.

## Small spelling changes

Most practical name corrections involve tiny changes the client can actually live with. Here are three common types applied to the same client. Each was verified letter by letter.

### Double letters

Adding a second L gives **Nikhill Mehra**.

- NIKHILL: 36 + 3 = 39 → 12 → 3. EX: 3 + 9 = 12 → **3**.
- No vowel changes, so SU stays **6**.

A doubled consonant only ever moves EX. It is the cleanest way to adjust Expression without touching Soul Urge.

### C/K swaps

Replacing K (2) with C (3) gives **Nichil Mehra**.

- NICHIL: N5 I9 C3 H8 I9 L3 = 37 → 10 → 1. EX: 1 + 9 = 10 → **1**.
- SU stays **6**.

The swap adds exactly one to the first name's total. That predictable shift makes C/K and similar swaps useful for testing.

### Vowel shifts

Replacing the second I with EE gives **Nikheel Mehra**.

- NIKHEEL: N5 I9 K2 H8 E5 E5 L3 = 37 → 10 → 1. EX: 1 + 9 = 10 → **1**.
- Vowels: I9 E5 E5 = 19 → 10 → 1. SU: 1 + 6 = **7**.

A vowel shift moves both EX and SU at once. Watch the compound totals too. The first-name vowel total here is 19, which many practitioners read as a [karmic debt number](/learn/karmic-debt-numbers) at the name-part level. Decide whether your method flags karmic compounds within name parts or only in the final total.

### Summary of options

| Version | EX | SU | Change type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nikhil Mehra (current) | 9 | 6 | — |
| Nikhill Mehra | 3 | 6 | Double letter |
| Nichil Mehra | 1 | 6 | C/K swap |
| Nikheel Mehra | 1 | 7 | Vowel shift |

Notice that the C/K swap and the vowel shift both produce EX 1, matching the BD. Only the vowel shift changes the SU. Whether EX 1 or EX 3 suits a Life Path 5 depends on your friendship table. The same calculation will be read differently by different schools, which is exactly why your conventions need writing down.

## Presenting options to clients

How you present a name correction matters as much as the arithmetic.

### Offer a shortlist, not a verdict

Give the client two or three viable options, never one. For each, show the new EX and SU and a plain explanation of what changes. Let the client say how each version looks and feels when written. A name they dislike will not be used, however good its numbers.

### Let the client choose

Make it clear the decision is theirs. Explain the trade-offs honestly. For example, a vowel shift may change pronunciation, while a double letter usually does not.

### Practical adoption

Most clients adopt a corrected spelling in everyday use first:

- **Signature:** the most personal and frequent use of the name.
- **Email display name:** seen by colleagues and clients every day.
- **Social profiles and business cards:** the public-facing version of the name.

Advise consistency. A spelling used in some places and not others sends a mixed message.

### Legal name change is the client's decision

Changing a name on official documents is a significant legal and administrative step. It is entirely the client's choice. Never present it as necessary. Your role is to describe the numerological picture; the client weighs the practical consequences.

## Key points

- Compare EX and SU against BD and LP using one documented friendship table.
- Check for over-energy before judging harmony.
- Double letters move EX only; vowel shifts move both EX and SU.
- Check compound totals in each name part for karmic debt patterns.
- Offer a shortlist, let the client choose, and leave legal changes to them.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I always recommend a name change?

No. If the name numbers already harmonise reasonably well with the birth numbers, say so. Recommending unnecessary changes undermines trust.

### Does a nickname count?

Many practitioners analyse both the birth name and the name in daily use. Decide which your method treats as primary and explain this to the client.

### How many letters can I change?

Keep changes small enough that the name stays recognisable and pronounceable. One or two letters is usual in practice.
