Reduction looks like the easiest part of numerology: add the digits until one remains. Yet most disagreements between practitioners come down to one question — when do you stop? This article sets out a clear rule set for reducing, keeping master numbers and flagging karmic debt, works through the edge cases that cause confusion, and ends with a checklist you can apply to every chart.

## Reduction rules

In Pythagorean numerology, a multi-digit number is reduced by adding its digits. You repeat the process until you reach a single digit from 1 to 9, unless a stopping rule applies.

1. **Add the digits.** 47 becomes 4 + 7 = 11. 1990 becomes 1 + 9 + 9 + 0 = 19.
2. **Check the result.** Before reducing again, look at the number you have reached.
3. **Stop at a master number** (11, 22 or 33) in a core chart position.
4. **Note karmic debt** (13, 14, 16 or 19) if the compound total equals one of them, then continue reducing.
5. **Continue** until you reach a single digit or a master number.

Pause at every stage. A compound such as 38 passes through 11 on its way down, so you cannot jump straight to the end.

### Compound totals are not all equal

Most compounds, such as 25 → 7, carry no flag. Only master numbers, which stop reduction, and karmic debt numbers, which are recorded but still reduced, trigger a rule.

## Master numbers

The master numbers are 11, 22 and 33. When a core chart position reaches one of them, you stop and record it as 11/2, 22/4 or 33/6.

A master number can be reached directly or through a larger compound. A Life Path total of 29 reduces to 11. So do 38 and 47. In each case, the master number is present and the position is read as 11/2.

Master numbers also appear inside components, such as a day of 22, the month of November or a year like 1984 that totals 22. This library keeps them at the component level too. For a full treatment, see our [master numbers reference](/learn/master-numbers-11-22-33).

## Karmic debt

The karmic debt numbers are 13, 14, 16 and 19. Unlike master numbers, they do not stop reduction. You record the compound and carry on, writing the result as 13/4, 14/5, 16/7 or 19/1.

Karmic debt is flagged only when the compound total equals one of these numbers before the final reduction. A Life Path compound of 31 reduces to 4, but it is not 13/4, because 13 never appeared. The 4 is clean.

Traditional themes are: 13 for work and discipline, 14 for freedom and moderation, 16 for ego and rebuilding, and 19 for power and self-reliance. Our [karmic debt numbers guide](/learn/karmic-debt-numbers) covers interpretation.

## Edge cases

Most charts are straightforward. These are the situations that cause real disagreement, and how this library handles each.

### Component versus full-digit reduction

For dates, our house convention reduces day, month and year separately, keeping master numbers, then adds and reduces. The alternative adds all digits at once.

The two methods usually agree on the final digit but can disagree on the compound. Take a fictional client born 9 December 1979.

| Method | Working | Compound | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Component | 9 + (1 + 2 = 3) + (1 + 9 + 7 + 9 = 26 → 8) = 20 | 20 | 2 |
| Full-digit | 0 + 9 + 1 + 2 + 1 + 9 + 7 + 9 = 38 → 11 | 38 | 11/2 |

The component method gives Life Path 2. The full-digit method gives 11/2. The same applies to our sample client Priya Sharma, born 15 August 1990, who is 15/6 by components and 33/6 by full digits. Choose one method and keep it.

### 11 in the day: born on the 29th

A client born on the 29th has a day of 2 + 9 = 11. In the Birthday position, this is read as 11/2. In the Life Path, this library keeps the 11 as a component before adding.

Clients born on the 11th or 22nd carry a master Birthday directly. The 29th is the only other day that reduces to a master number. No day of the month reduces to 33.

### A day that is karmic debt

A client born on the 13th, 14th, 16th or 19th carries karmic debt in the Birthday position. Reduce the day to its single digit for the Life Path calculation. Record the karmic debt separately, so it is not lost.

### Names that total 22 in one part

Name calculations raise a similar question. Our house convention mirrors the component method for dates: reduce each name part separately, keeping 11, 22 or 33 and noting 13, 14, 16 or 19, then add the reduced parts and reduce again. Consider the fictional name Deepa Shah.

| Name | Letters and values | Total | Kept as |
|---|---|---|---|
| DEEPA | D 4, E 5, E 5, P 7, A 1 | 22 | 22 (master) |
| SHAH | S 1, H 8, A 1, H 8 | 18 | 1 + 8 = 9 |

By the house convention, 22 + 9 = 31 → 3 + 1 = 4. **Expression 31/4**, with a master 22 noted in the first name.

Two alternatives are also in use:

| Approach | Working | Compound | Expression |
|---|---|---|---|
| **House: reduce each part, keep master** | 22 + 9 | 31 | 4 |
| Alternative: total all letters at once | 22 + 18 | 40 | 4 |
| Alternative: reduce each part fully | (2 + 2 = 4) + 9 | 13 | 13/4 |

All three end in 4. Only the last produces a karmic debt flag, because reducing DEEPA's 22 to 4 creates a compound of 13. Totalling all letters at once loses sight of the master 22 in the first name. Whatever you choose, write it down and apply it to every client.

### Compound totals that reduce to a master number

A compound of 38 reduces to 11. A compound of 47 also reduces to 11. Both are read as 11/2. You may write the full path, such as 38/11/2, so a client or colleague can see how you reached it.

The same logic applies to 22 and 33, though only very large compounds, such as 499 (4 + 9 + 9 = 22), reach them this way.

### Personal Year and other cycles

Personal Years follow the same reduction rules. Some practitioners keep master Personal Years such as 11/2; others always reduce to a single digit. If you keep them, also check for karmic compounds. For Priya Sharma, the Personal Year for 2027 is 8 + 6 + 11 = 25 → 7, a clean 7.

## A decision checklist

Use this checklist for every number in a core chart. It applies to Life Path, Expression, Soul Urge, Personality, Birthday and cycle numbers alike.

1. **Confirm your inputs.** Check the full date of birth or the exact spelling of the name the client wants read.
2. **Confirm your method.** The house convention uses components for dates and per-name-part totals for names. If you use another method, write it down.
3. **Reduce components first, if your method uses them.** Stop at 11, 22 or 33 within a component.
4. **Add to reach the compound total.** Record this number before reducing it.
5. **Is the compound 13, 14, 16 or 19?** If yes, flag karmic debt and continue reducing.
6. **Is the compound 11, 22 or 33?** If yes, stop. Record it as 11/2, 22/4 or 33/6.
7. **Is the compound larger than 33?** Reduce once and return to step 5 with the new number.
8. **Is the result a single digit?** If yes, stop. This is the final number.
9. **Record the full path.** For example, 38/11/2 or 14/5, so the reasoning is visible.
10. **Cross-check.** For names, confirm that Soul Urge plus Personality reduces to the Expression digit. For dates, recheck the year's digits.

Steps 5 and 6 come before any further reduction. That order is what protects the master and karmic flags.

## Key points

- Pause at every stage of reduction; do not jump to the final digit.
- Master numbers (11, 22, 33) stop reduction in core chart positions.
- Karmic debt (13, 14, 16, 19) is flagged but still reduced to 4, 5, 7 or 1.
- Component and full-digit methods can give different compounds for the same date.
- For names, reduce each part, keep masters, then add; other methods shift the flags.
- Choose your conventions, write them down and apply them to every chart.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 31 a karmic debt number?

No. 31 reduces to 4, but karmic debt is flagged only when the compound itself is 13, 14, 16 or 19. A Life Path of 31/4 is a clean 4.

### Can a number be both master and karmic?

Not at the same stage. A compound is either 11, 22 or 33, or it is 13, 14, 16 or 19. A chart can contain both, but in different positions or components.

### What if my client's other numerologist uses a different method?

Explain that methods differ and show your working. Your result is valid within your method. Changing method mid-reading to match another practitioner undermines both readings.
