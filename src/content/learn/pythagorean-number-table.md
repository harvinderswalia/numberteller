Every name calculation in Pythagorean numerology rests on one small table. If a single letter is misread, every number built from it is wrong, and a name correction built on that error will be wrong too. This reference sets out the table, explains how vowels and Y are handled, and gives a worked example with a built-in cross-check you can use in every consultation.

## The letter table

The Pythagorean system assigns the 26 letters of the Latin alphabet to the numbers 1 to 9 in order. A is 1, I is 9, J starts again at 1, and so on until Z, which falls on 8.

| Number | Letters |
|---|---|
| 1 | A, J, S |
| 2 | B, K, T |
| 3 | C, L, U |
| 4 | D, M, V |
| 5 | E, N, W |
| 6 | F, O, X |
| 7 | G, P, Y |
| 8 | H, Q, Z |
| 9 | I, R |

Two features are worth committing to memory. First, the numbers 1 to 8 each carry three letters, while 9 carries only two. Second, the table is sequential, so you can rebuild it from scratch by writing the alphabet in rows of nine.

A quick way to find any letter: take its position in the alphabet and reduce it. K is the 11th letter, and 1 + 1 = 2. T is the 20th, and 2 + 0 = 2. Some practitioners note that K (11) and V (22) carry master vibrations at the letter level. That is an interpretive layer, not a change to the value used in calculation: K is still counted as 2 and V as 4.

## Vowels and consonants

The table gives each letter its value. The vowel and consonant split decides which letters go into which number.

- **Expression** (also called Destiny) uses every letter of the name.
- **Soul Urge** (also called Heart's Desire) uses the vowels.
- **Personality** uses the consonants.

The vowels are A, E, I, O and U. Every other letter, including Y and W, is a consonant. This is how NumberTeller calculates Soul Urge and Personality. Y always has the value 7 and always goes into Personality.

## Worked example: Expression, Soul Urge and Personality

Our sample client is Kiran Joshi, a fictional name with no Y to complicate the vowel split.

The house convention in this library mirrors our component method for Life Path. Reduce each name part separately to a single digit, keeping 11, 22 or 33 and noting any 13, 14, 16 or 19 compound. Then add the reduced parts and reduce again, keeping master numbers and flagging karmic debt on the final compound.

### Step 1: Expression (all letters, per name)

| Name | Letters and values | Total | Reduced |
|---|---|---|---|
| KIRAN | K 2, I 9, R 9, A 1, N 5 | 26 | 2 + 6 = 8 |
| JOSHI | J 1, O 6, S 1, H 8, I 9 | 25 | 2 + 5 = 7 |

8 + 7 = 15 → 1 + 5 = 6. **Expression 15/6.**

### Step 2: Soul Urge (vowels, per name)

| Name | Vowels | Total | Reduced |
|---|---|---|---|
| KIRAN | I 9, A 1 | 10 | 1 |
| JOSHI | O 6, I 9 | 15 | 6 |

1 + 6 = 7. **Soul Urge 7.**

### Step 3: Personality (consonants, per name)

| Name | Consonants | Total | Reduced |
|---|---|---|---|
| KIRAN | K 2, R 9, N 5 | 16 | 7 (karmic 16 noted) |
| JOSHI | J 1, S 1, H 8 | 10 | 1 |

7 + 1 = 8. **Personality 8.**

The consonants of KIRAN total 16, a karmic debt compound at name-part level. Note it in the chart as a supporting detail. The final Personality is a clean 8.

### Summary

| Number | House convention | All letters at once |
|---|---|---|
| Expression | 15/6 | 51/6 |
| Soul Urge | 7 | 25/7 |
| Personality | 8 (16 in KIRAN) | 26/8 |

### The all-at-once alternative

Some schools add every letter of the full name in one pass. For Kiran, that gives 26 + 25 = 51 → 6 for Expression, 25 → 7 for Soul Urge and 26 → 8 for Personality.

The final digits agree here, but the compounds differ: 15 against 51. Because master numbers and karmic debt are read from compounds, the two methods can flag different numbers for the same name. Pick one convention and apply it to every chart. Our rule set on [when to stop reducing numbers](/learn/when-to-stop-reducing-numbers) explains how to handle name parts that total 11, 22 or 33.

## Checking your work

Letter-value errors are easy to make and hard to spot by eye. Fortunately, the Pythagorean method contains its own check.

Every letter is either a vowel or a consonant. So vowels and consonants together must account for every letter. Under the house convention, check this at the reduced level:

**Soul Urge + Personality, reduced to a single digit, = Expression reduced to a single digit.**

For Kiran Joshi: 7 + 8 = 15 → 6, and Expression is 6. The check holds.

Compare base digits, not compounds. Per-name reduction changes the compounds, so Soul Urge and Personality compounds will not always add up to the Expression compound. Treat 11, 22 and 33 as 2, 4 and 6 for this check only. With the all-at-once method, the compounds themselves balance: 25 + 26 = 51.

If the two sides do not match, you have missed a letter, assigned a wrong value, or counted a letter in both groups. Go back through the name letter by letter.

### A routine for every name

1. Write the name exactly as the client uses it, in capitals, letter by letter.
2. Write the value under each letter from the table.
3. Mark each letter V or C, applying your Y rule.
4. For each name part, total all letters, the vowels and the consonants.
5. Reduce each part, keeping 11, 22 or 33 and noting 13, 14, 16 or 19.
6. Add the reduced parts and reduce again, checking the final compound.
7. Confirm that Soul Urge plus Personality reduces to the Expression digit.

This takes a minute longer than working from memory. It also catches the most common errors before they reach your client.

### Errors the check will not catch

The cross-check proves internal consistency, not correctness. If you use the wrong letter value consistently, both sides still balance. The most frequent offenders are letters near group boundaries: R is 9, not 1; S is 1; Z is 8. If a result surprises you, recheck those letters against the table.

The check also cannot tell you whether you have the right name. Confirm the spelling, and whether the client wants their birth name, current name or a professional name read. Our guide to [common numerology calculation errors](/learn/common-numerology-calculation-errors) lists further pitfalls.

## Pythagorean vs Chaldean

The Chaldean system uses a different table, assigning letters to 1–8 based on sound rather than alphabetical order, and it treats 9 as sacred and does not assign it to a letter. Many Chaldean practitioners also give more weight to the name in daily use. The two tables give different results for most names, so never mix them in one chart. This library uses the Pythagorean table throughout.

## Key points

- The Pythagorean table runs A = 1 to I = 9, then repeats; Z = 8.
- Expression uses all letters, Soul Urge the vowels, Personality the consonants.
- Y is always 7 and is counted as a consonant.
- House convention: reduce each name part, then add the parts and reduce again.
- Soul Urge plus Personality must reduce to the same digit as Expression.
- Kiran Joshi: Expression 15/6, Soul Urge 7, Personality 8, and 7 + 8 = 15 → 6.
- Never mix Pythagorean and Chaldean values in one chart.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does 9 have only two letters?

There are 26 letters and nine numbers. After two full rows of nine (A to R), the third row has only eight letters (S to Z), so 9 receives just I and R.

### Should I include middle names?

Include every name on the version of the name you are reading. If the client uses a middle name on official documents, it belongs in the birth-name Expression. Note your choice in the chart.

### Does the cross-check work with the all-at-once method?

Yes, and more strictly: the unreduced Soul Urge and Personality compounds add up exactly to the Expression compound. Under per-name reduction, compare single digits instead.
