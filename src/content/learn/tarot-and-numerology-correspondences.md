Every tarot card carries a number, which makes tarot a natural partner for numerology. Many practitioners use cards to illustrate a chart, or numbers to anchor a spread. This guide sets out the correspondences, the conventions that differ between decks, and a method for linking cards to a client's Personal Year.

## Why numbers connect the two systems

The Major Arcana are numbered 0 to 21. The Minor Arcana pip cards are numbered Ace (1) to 10 in each suit. Reduce those numbers in the Pythagorean way and every card links to a root from 1 to 9, and sometimes to a master number or karmic debt compound.

The connection is a correspondence, not an identity. The Star and Personal Year 8 do not mean the same thing. They share a number, which gives you a bridge between two languages.

## Conventions that differ

Decks and schools do not agree on every number. Choose one convention, state it to the client and apply it every time.

### The Fool: 0 or 22

The Fool is numbered 0 in Rider–Waite. In the Marseille tradition it is often unnumbered. Practitioners handle it in three common ways:

1. **As 0.** The Fool stands for pure potential and has no reduced value. It is read for meaning but left out of sums.
2. **As 22.** The Fool is placed after The World (21) as the 22nd card. It is then treated as 22/4, which some readers link to the master builder.
3. **As 22 reduced to 4.** Some schools use 22 but do not keep master numbers in tarot sums.

This library suggests option 1 for spread sums and option 2 when you discuss the card's numerological character. Whatever you choose, write it down.

### Strength and Justice

In the Rider–Waite deck, Strength is 8 and Justice is 11. Waite swapped them from the older order to fit his astrological scheme.

In the Marseille tradition, Justice is 8 and Strength (Force) is 11. The Thoth deck also places Adjustment (its Justice) at 8 and Lust (its Strength) at 11.

The swap matters. In Rider–Waite, Justice carries the master number 11/2. In Marseille or Thoth, the Strength card carries it. Always use the numbers printed on the deck your client sees.

## The Minor Arcana

### Pip cards

The pip cards in each suit run from Ace to 10:

| Card | Number | Numerology number |
|---|---|---|
| Ace | 1 | 1 |
| Two to Nine | 2–9 | 2–9 |
| Ten | 10 | 10 → 1 |

The Ten is often read as completion leading to a new cycle, which fits its reduction to 1. Many readers link pip numbers directly to Personal Year themes: Fives with change, Fours with structure, Nines with completion.

### Suits and elements

Each suit is commonly linked to an element: Wands with Fire, Cups with Water, Swords with Air and Pentacles with Earth. Some schools swap Wands and Swords. These are tarot elements and do not match the five elements of the Lo Shu tradition, so do not mix the two systems in one remedy.

### Court cards

Court cards have no printed number. Some readers assign Page 11, Knight 12, Queen 13 and King 14. Others leave court cards out of numerical work entirely.

If you assign numbers, be careful. A Queen at 13 or a King at 14 is not a karmic debt reading. Many practitioners therefore leave courts unnumbered, which avoids the confusion.

## Linking cards to the Personal Year

The simplest bridge is direct: find the Major Arcana that share the client's Personal Year root. Treat the card with the single-digit number as the main "year card" and the compound card as its shadow or depth.

Our fictional sample client, Priya Sharma, was born on 15 August 1990. Her Personal Year for 2027 is 8 + 6 + 11 = 25/7, as explained in our [Personal Year guide](/learn/personal-year-numbers).

- **Main year card:** The Chariot (7) – direction, discipline, steering inner forces.
- **Compound card:** The Tower (16/7) – reviewing structures that no longer hold.

This pairing suits a 7 year well. The Chariot keeps the reflective year purposeful; The Tower reminds the client that honest review can mean rebuilding.

Some readers instead add the birth month, birth day and forecast year as whole numbers. For Priya that is 8 + 15 + 2027 = 2050, and 2 + 0 + 5 + 0 = 7. Here it also gives The Chariot. The methods will not always agree, so pick one.

## Worked example: a three-card spread

Priya draws three Major Arcana cards for 2027 in a past–present–future spread.

| Position | Card | Card number | Numerology number |
|---|---|---|---|
| Past | The Hermit | 9 | 9 |
| Present | The Empress | 3 | 3 |
| Future | The Sun | 19 | 19/1 (karmic debt) |
| **Sum** | | 9 + 3 + 19 = 31 | 3 + 1 = **4** |

**Reading the numbers:**

1. **The Hermit (9)** in the past fits a cycle of completion and withdrawal before the current phase.
2. **The Empress (3)** in the present suggests expression and creativity, a lighter note within her reflective 7 year.
3. **The Sun (19/1)** in the future is bright, but its compound 19 is traditionally linked to independence versus self-reliance. A good bridge sentence: "success comes through standing on your own, while still accepting help."
4. **The spread sum, 4,** points to structure and steady work as the thread that connects the three cards.

Notice that no card matches the Personal Year 7 directly. That is useful in itself. It suggests the year's outward events may feel more creative and visible than its inner theme of reflection.

Before using any spread sum, decide whether you keep master numbers in tarot sums. Our guide on [when to stop reducing numbers](/learn/when-to-stop-reducing-numbers) explains the choice.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to use the Rider–Waite numbering?

No. Use the numbering printed on the deck you read with, and tell the client which convention you follow.

### Is Death (13) a sign of karmic debt in a reading?

The number 13 does correspond to the karmic debt compound 13/4, associated with work and discipline. Treat it as a numerical bridge, not a separate karmic debt diagnosis.

### Should I add Minor Arcana cards to spread sums?

Some practitioners add pip numbers and leave out court cards. Others sum only the Major Arcana. Either works if applied consistently.
